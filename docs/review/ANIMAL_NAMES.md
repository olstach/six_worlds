## Naga  `naga`


**How this birth names**

<!--@ animal_realm_names.json | ocean.births.naga | naming_philosophy -->
Naga hold lineage as the deepest form of identity — a name is an inheritance, a claim, a record. Children are named for what the parent hopes they will carry into the future: a current, a memory, a way of holding still. Naga names are fluid, vowel-heavy, sinuous in the mouth. A naga who has held the same thermal current for a century may rename themselves after it. Place names are events remembered: not where something is, but what happened there and who was present.

## Karka  `karka`


**How this birth names**

<!--@ animal_realm_names.json | ocean.births.karka | naming_philosophy -->
Karka value endurance above all things — what is built, what is held, what does not crack. Names are given late, after the child's shell has begun to harden into its characteristic pattern. A name given too early is considered bad luck: you have named a thing before it has proven itself. Karka parents say: 'May nothing crack you.' Place names are functional and precise — defined by thermal output, structural integrity, depth to bedrock.
<!--@end-->

## Matsya  `matsya`


**How this birth names**

<!--@ animal_realm_names.json | ocean.births.matsya | naming_philosophy -->
Matsya are the most present-tense of all births — they live in the immediate movement of the shoal, in the collective turn. Individual names are given by the shoal, not the parents, and usually describe the child's behavior in their first week. A matsya who always turns left at the decision point becomes Leftward. A matsya who sees the shadow first becomes Shadowwatch. Names are short because they need to carry fast across water.
<!--@end-->

## Makara  `makara`


**How this birth names**

<!--@ animal_realm_names.json | ocean.births.makara | naming_philosophy -->
Makara are so ancient and solitary that their naming traditions are barely traditions — more like geological habits. They rarely name children; often a young makara goes unnamed until they have held a territory for a season, at which point they name themselves after its most characteristic feature. Place names are simple, definitive, and never change. 'The Trench' has been the Trench for longer than anything else in the ocean has a name.

## Uluka  `uluka`


**How this birth names**

<!--@ animal_realm_names.json | sky_distributed.births.uluka | naming_philosophy -->
Uluka are the most deliberate namers of all births. They watch a child for a full season before naming. What is observed in that season becomes the name — not what is hoped, but what is seen. 'You cannot name what you haven't watched,' they say. Place names are named for specific events of perception: what was heard there, what was seen from there, what was understood in that location for the first time.
<!--@end-->
