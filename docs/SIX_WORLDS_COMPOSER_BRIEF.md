# Six Worlds — A Composer's Brief

*Everything you need to get a feel for the game, its worlds, its characters, and the emotional territory the music will live in. Prepared for our composer. No coding knowledge assumed — this is a picture of the world, not a spec sheet.*

> **One-line pitch:** A tactical RPG roguelike set in the six realms of Tibetan Buddhist cosmology, where you die, are reborn according to your hidden karma, and travel upward through hell, the realm of hungry ghosts, the animal world, and beyond — one lifetime at a time.

> **Note:** A formatted Word version of this document (`Six_Worlds_Composer_Brief.docx`) lives alongside this file in `docs/` — that's the copy to hand to the composer.

---

## 1. The Vision & The Feel

**Six Worlds** is a passion project — a turn-based tactical RPG (think *Final Fantasy Tactics* or *Disgaea* grid combat) wrapped in a roguelike journey (think *FTL*'s branching encounters and one-more-run structure). But its soul is Buddhist. The mechanics *are* the philosophy:

- **Reincarnation, not respawning.** When you die, you are reborn into one of the six realms — chosen by karma you accumulated but were never allowed to see. Every run is a life.
- **No levels, no fanfare.** No triumphant "level up!" moments. Experience is a quiet currency spent gradually, reflecting slow cultivation rather than sudden power spikes. Progress is a long, patient climb.
- **Karma is invisible.** The player never sees a morality meter. Choices ripple outward silently and only reveal themselves at the moment of death and rebirth.
- **The journey is upward.** You begin in Hell and travel toward the higher realms — a pilgrimage through suffering, craving, instinct, humanity, jealousy, and finally the divine.

### Visual aesthetic (so the music can match the eye)

The art direction is **Tibetan thangka painting meets classic pixel art**: ornate, gilded, sacred-geometry UI frames — deep temple reds, gold leaf, indigo — surrounding pixel-art characters and environments. Ceremonial and hand-made on the outside; intimate and lo-fi on the inside. The music likely wants that same tension: **real, resonant, ritual instrumentation** (Himalayan and broader Asian traditions — bells, bowls, horns, drones, hand percussion, plucked strings) meeting the **compact, loopable, game-scaled** demands of a roguelike heard for hours.

### Tone

| The game leans dark… | …but never nihilistic |
|---|---|
| Suffering, impermanence, hunger, cold cruelty, bureaucratic torment, the weight of old karma. | Compassion, wit, absurd humor, unexpected tenderness, the constant possibility of liberation. Even hell has teahouses. |

The writing voice is a key tonal signal: melancholy and mythic, but frequently *funny* — a paranoid warlord killed by a cabbage cart, a miser reborn hungry, a librarian who exploded from repressed frustration and "found the experience clarifying." The music shouldn't be relentlessly grim; it should hold sorrow and irony at once.

## 2. The Six Realms (the album's six "sides")

The cosmology is the *bhavachakra* — the Wheel of Life. Six realms, each its own world with its own biome, inhabitants, palette, and emotional register. The player travels them in ascending order. Three are fully built; three are designed but not yet implemented. Each realm is the strongest candidate for its own musical identity / suite.

### I. Naraka — The Hell Realm  *(built)*
*The realm of hatred and aggression.* Split by an impassable mountain range: the **frozen wastes of the north** (ice lakes, wailing frozen spirits, blue-black armored demons, glacial silence) and the **burning badlands of the south** (lava flows, forges, brimstone, ash). Populated by six colored devils. Towns named from *corpse, blood, darkness, fear, death, glacier, fire*. Boss: Yama's Lieutenant, guarding the portal onward.
**Musical territory:** two opposite colds and heats of the same suffering — the north wants sparse, ringing, sub-zero stillness and grief; the south wants heat-shimmer, forge-clang, low menace. And because the journey *starts* here, it can't be pure despair — there's grit, defiance, gallows humor.

### II. Pretaloka — The Hungry Ghost Realm  *(built)*
*The realm of insatiable craving.* Three zones: **fetid swamps** crawling with the newly dead (shambling, jerky, jiangshi-like corpses), **dry ancient graveyards** where cheerful conscious skeletons keep the catacombs, and the **charnel grounds** where vetalas (trickster undead) hold court in palaces that are *not quite real* — illusory feasts, glittering troves that dissolve when touched. Ruled by the Insatiable King. Bellies distended, throats constricted to a hair's width.
**Musical territory:** longing and emptiness; something that always reaches and never arrives. Swamp murk vs. dry rattling bone-percussion (the skeletons are oddly upbeat — turquoise-skirted jesters dance here) vs. seductive, hollow palace music that promises a feast and delivers ash. Illusion is the key idea: beauty with nothing behind it.

### III. Tiryakloka — The Animal Realm  *(built)*
*The realm of instinct, territory, and predation.* The most alive and least human. Three biomes: **ocean depths** (naga serpent-courts older than memory, ship-swallowing makara, crab-lords holding the reef passes), **ancient forest** (dark, layered, every fallen log a territorial claim — tigers, boars, wolf-packs, monkey-troops, shapeshifting rakshasa), and **open meadow** (charging mantis-warriors, sun-darkening bee-swarms, standing stones, migrations). Birds live everywhere but own the high places. Boss: the King of Beasts.
**Musical territory:** the widest range — submerged, ancient, sovereign underwater music; dense, humid, watchful forest music; vast, exposed, windswept plains. Less "civilization," more elemental and rhythmic — breath, pulse, swarm, tide.

### IV. Manuṣyaloka — The Human Realm  *(planned)*
*The realm of choice and balance* — and, in Buddhism, the only realm from which liberation is truly possible. Humans are "beings of potential." Dialogue-focused, morally complex, the realm where karmic stakes are highest. Tonally the pivot: the most grounded, the most bittersweet, the closest to us.

### V. Asuraloka — The Asura (Demigod) Realm  *(planned)*
*The realm of jealousy, competition, and endless war.* Titan-like beings who have everything the gods have except contentment, and who war eternally to seize the rest. Duels, martial spectacle, multi-armed warriors. Tonally: martial, proud, glorious-but-hollow — triumph that never satisfies.

### VI. Devaloka — The God Realm  *(planned)*
*The realm of the gods* — celestial musicians (gandharvas), heavenly dancers (apsaras), planetary deities. Blissful, radiant, and yet a *trap*: the gods are so comfortable they forget to seek liberation, and eventually fall. Note the resonance for your project — the gandharvas are literally the **celestial musician caste**. Tonally: transcendent, gorgeous, weightless — with a seed of impermanence, because even heaven ends.

> A structural idea worth knowing: the realms are not simply "worse → better." Each is a trap of a different flavor. Hell traps you in rage; the god realm traps you in bliss. The music could carry a through-line: *no realm is home; the wheel keeps turning.*

## 3. The Emotional Engine — Five Elements, Kleshas & Wisdoms

This is the single most useful system for a composer to understand — the game's emotional grammar, built on **five elements** (a natural five-color palette for leitmotifs). Each element carries an emotional energy that can express as a *klesha* (a "mental poison," when the mind is contracted) or as a *wisdom* (when the mind is open). **Same energy, opposite expression.** Characters have inner lives that drift between these poles based on what happens to them.

| Element | Klesha pole (dark) | Wisdom pole (bright) | Sonic color idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Space** | Ignorance, confusion, dissociation | Rigpa — spacious, open clarity | Emptiness, drone, silence, vast reverb |
| **Fire** | Desire, craving, obsession | Magnetism, discriminating appreciation | Heat, pulse, brass, crackle, allure |
| **Water** | Aversion — grief, fear, cold hatred | Mirror-like precision, compassion | Corrosive cold, weeping strings, drip, tide |
| **Earth** | Pride, arrogance, insecurity | Equanimity, groundedness | Weight, low drums, stone, unshakeable ostinato |
| **Air** | Jealousy, paranoia, sowing distrust | All-accomplishing energy, inspiration | Movement, flutter, high winds, nervous motion |

A crucial distinction: **Fire aggression is loud and passionate** (a punch, an outburst, a charge) while **Water aggression is cold and devious** (a poison, a cutting remark, trust corroded quietly). They share the aggressive register but are opposite in texture — hot vs. cold violence.

In play, party members accumulate emotional "pressure" in each element and cross thresholds into named states:

| Element | Descending into klesha → | ← Rising into wisdom |
|---|---|---|
| Space | Confused → Dissociated → **Absent** | Clear-headed → Open → **Rigpa** |
| Fire | Restless → Craving → **Consumed** | Warm → Magnetizing → **Radiant** |
| Water | Irritable → Grief-struck → **Poisonous** | Focused → Clear-eyed → **Compassionate** |
| Earth | Insecure → Arrogant → **Humiliated** | Grounded → Equanimous → **Unshakeable** |
| Air | Anxious → Paranoid → **Envious** | Alert → Inspired → **Brilliant** |

At the extremes a character can spontaneously act — a berserker "consumed" by fire disappears to gamble; a "radiant" leader gives a rallying speech that fires up the whole party. **The whole system rests on the idea that darkness and wisdom are the same note played two ways** — a beautiful thing to compose around (imagine a single theme that can curdle or bloom).

## 4. The Elements as Magic & Color

Beyond emotion, the five elements (plus specializations) organize the game's **35 skills** and **300+ spells**. Established colors/themes:

| Element | Color | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Space | Violet | Clarity, emptiness, consciousness, dharma |
| Air | Pale cyan | Movement, intellect, playfulness |
| Fire | Orange | Passion, direct action, willpower |
| Water | Blue | Fluidity, transformation, healing |
| Earth | Tan | Stability, materialism, endurance |

There are also **hidden "domain" schools** — magic at the intersection of two elements, learned only at rare secret locations (each has its own one-of-a-kind event; see the appendix). Each is pure atmosphere, a tiny genre of its own. All ten, because they add so much to the vibe:

| Domain | Elements | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound** | Air + Space | Vibration, mantra, resonance. Battle chants, dirges, sonic booms — and "the legendary eleventh note that transcends all structure." Found at **The Court of the Musician Prince**. |
| **Crystal** | Earth + Space | Clarity, resonance, precision. Earth compressed to perfection: reveals what hides, focuses the mind to a razor edge, defends by refracting light rather than absorbing it. |
| **Glass** | Earth + Air | Earth seized by air and made impossibly thin — transparent, silent, lethal when broken. Precise and reactive: a shard in the dark, powder in the eyes, a globe that waits to shatter. |
| **Smoke** | Earth + Fire | Concealment, suffocation, transformation. Neither solid nor flame — it lingers, blinds, creeps into lungs and minds. "The Smoking Mirror": a surface that shows what the world hides. |
| **Mud** | Earth + Water | Corruption, weight, tenacity. The endurance of earth with the corrosion of water — patient, heavy, suffocating; slow but relentless, hard to wash off. |
| **Cloud** | Water + Air | Transience, sovereignty, height. Slips past barriers, sheds damage like rain, carries the party home on a breath of wind. The sovereign nagas who dwell between the rain and the stars. |
| **Steam** | Water + Fire | Purification and eruption. Healing mists that strip poison from allies; geysers that hurl enemies skyward; lingering fog that sears those who linger. |
| **Rainbow** | Space + White | Prismatic chaos, revelation. Every spell rolls its own dice — blind, charm, heal, or burn. To master it is to see reality itself as a display of light. |
| **Tummo** | Fire + Air | Inner yogic heat — the flame that melts the crown and blazes up the secret channels. Transforms the caster rather than the world. |
| **Luminosity** | Fire + Space | The clear light of awareness itself — not sun or flame but the light of the mind. Vision that pierces all deception; illusions so vivid they fool those who know better. |

## 5. Who You Play — Races & Births

You don't pick a race at a menu — you're *reborn* into one based on karma. Each realm has its own peoples.

**Hell — the Six Devils.** Colored by temperament, each tied to an element: **Red** (rage & lust, hell's finest warriors, fire), **Blue** (cold, grudge-keeping, water), **Yellow** (greedy, proud, earth), **Green** (quick-witted, paranoid operatives, air), **Black** (spawned from the Outer Dark, feed on fear, space), **White** (lazy, ditzy, oddly pleasant company).

**Hungry Ghost — the Restless Dead.** **Rolang** (newly-dead shamblers), **Preserved** (alchemically halted from decay, aware and dangerous), **Skeletons** in five metals (silver healers, copper sorcerers, golden nobles, iron soldiers, turquoise jester-dancers who "prance cheerfully in rainbow skirts"), **Vetala** (brilliant trickster undead), **Dré** (near-dissolved ghosts), **Gyelpo & Dralha** (sorcerer-kings and warrior-kings who refused to relinquish power at death), **Shaza** (predators hunting bodies to devour), **Yidag** (the classic hungry ghost — insatiable, constricted throat).

**Animal — the Births** (each covers a whole family of creatures). Named from Sanskrit myth: **Naga** (wise, sovereign water-serpents), **Yaksha & Rakshasa** (meadow and forest nature-lords), **Matsya / Makara / Karka** (fish, sea-monsters, armored crustaceans), **Bhramara** (hive insects — "the colony thinks through them"), **Shardula** (tigers & fast predators), **Varaha** (boars & heavies), **Gana** (pack & herd animals), **Vanara** (Hanuman's clever monkey-folk), and the birds — **Shyena** (raptors), **Kapota** (swift messenger birds), **Uluka** (owls & uncanny talking birds, omens).

Higher realms (Human, Asura, God) have their peoples sketched — humans and nomads; tsen and rudra titans; gandharvas, apsaras, planetary deities — but aren't fleshed out yet.

## 7. Moment-to-Moment: What the Player Is Actually Doing

Two main modes alternate, wanting very different music:

- **Overworld exploration** — a *Heroes of Might & Magic*-style top-down map: travel tile by tile, manage food and supplies, reveal fog of war, discover shrines/shops/teahouses/monster camps/story encounters, head toward the boss guarding the portal onward. This is the **ambient bed** — long, atmospheric, per-realm music the player lives inside. It must loop gracefully and not fatigue.
- **FTL-style encounters** — branching text events with safe/skill-gated/dice-roll choices, each quietly shifting karma. Little narrative set-pieces — a demon patrol, ice spirits guarding sleepers below a frozen lake, a suspicious gift. Tonally tense, eerie, or wry.
- **Tactical grid combat** — turn-based: initiative, movement, terrain (fire/ice/poison ground, height, cover), spells across ten schools, status effects, summons. The **active, driving** music — sustaining a tense multi-turn engagement, flexing between skirmish and boss/elite fight.
- **Rest & camp** — telling stories, reciting mantras, playing *night music* (a real in-game action that calms the party's emotional pressure), tending wounds. Home for the game's quietest, most restorative, most human music.

## 8. Recurring Places & Textures

Location-types that appear in all realms — each could carry a small recurring cue "re-skinned" per realm:

- **Teahouses & rest spots** — safety, warmth, a held breath.
- **Shrines, stupas, prayer flags, offering bowls** — the sacred intruding on the profane; small moments of grace.
- **Guilds & temples** (Temple of the Naga, Temple of the Eternal Fire, Garuda Roost, Hidden Gompa, Circle of the Yoginis, Black Lodge…) — where you learn spells; each has an elemental flavor.
- **Mercenary guilds & markets** — commerce, bustle, a rogues'-gallery energy.
- **Secret domain sites** (the Court of the Musician Prince, the Smoking Mirror, the Cloud Palace, Skygazing Gompa…) — rare, wondrous, one-of-a-kind.

## 10. Existing Sound & Practical Notes

- **Sound effects already exist** — a full library of combat/UI/spell SFX (weapon hits, spell casts and impacts, buffs, pickups, UI clicks) is wired in. What's essentially absent is **music** — the open canvas you'd be filling.
- **Engine: Godot 4.3.** Music is typically delivered as **OGG (Vorbis)** loops; the engine handles seamless looping and layering well. Stems/layers are welcome for adaptive/vertical approaches — e.g. an exploration bed that adds tension layers, or emotional-state layers tracking the klesha/wisdom system. Not required; simple loops are fine to start.
- **Loop-friendliness matters.** Roguelikes are long. Exploration and combat beds especially need to breathe and repeat without wearing out.
- **Instrumentation is wide open** — bells, singing bowls, dungchen horns, gyaling, damaru, hand drums, drones, chant, plucked strings — but the pixel-art side means it needn't be purist. Hybrid, atmospheric, and emotionally true beats authentic-but-lifeless. Follow the feeling.
- **Rights:** the project's content is shared non-commercially (a passion project, not for sale) — worth a conversation with Olaf about credit and licensing before digging in.

> **If you take away three things:** (1) it's Buddhist cosmology as a game — reincarnation, hidden karma, gradual cultivation, six distinct worlds. (2) The tone holds sorrow and wit together; don't play it as pure grimdark. (3) The five elements — and their klesha↔wisdom duality — are the emotional palette; a theme that can curdle into poison or bloom into wisdom would sit at the very heart of what this game is about.

## Appendix — A Walk Through the Events

This section exists so you can see **how encounters actually work** and soak up the writing voice. The game currently has **238 events** across the three built realms; below is a curated cross-section (~28) chosen for variety and vibe rather than completeness — the full sets live in the game's data files if you ever want them all.

Every encounter opens with a short scene, then offers a mix of three choice types (the color-coding is real — it's how they appear in-game):

- **Grey** — always available. The basic, human options: fight, walk away, pay, ask.
- **Blue** — unlocked only if a party member has the required skill or attribute. Always beneficial, always flavorful — this is where party composition and the skill system pay off. (Any one companion meeting the bar unlocks it.)
- **Yellow** — a dice roll (d20 + your best party attribute vs. a difficulty). Risk and tension; separate success/failure outcomes.

Almost every choice quietly shifts **karma** across the realms — invisibly — which is what eventually decides your next rebirth. Note how often the "enlightened" option (meditate, show mercy, see clearly) and the "grasping" option (steal, kill, take) sit side by side. That tension is the whole game in miniature.

### Naraka — Hell

**Demon Patrol** — *Three demons in frost-crusted armor block the path. Their captain raises a jagged halberd: "No one passes without the Warden's permission."*
- [Grey] Draw your weapon and attack. → combat
- [Blue] *(Charm 15)* Convince them you serve the Warden.
- [Yellow] *(Finesse roll)* Throw a bag of gold and slip past while they argue among themselves.
- [Grey] Offer them gold to look the other way.
- [Blue] *(Daggers 3)* Strike fast and precisely — take them down before they can sound the alarm.

**Ice Spirits** — *Translucent figures drift above a frozen lake, trapped in an eternal dance. One turns to you: "Do not disturb those dreaming below."*
- [Grey] Force your way through. → combat
- [Blue] *(Water Magic 3)* Speak to them in the language of water and cold.
- [Blue] *(Yoga 2)* Sit in meditation and commune with the spirits.
- [Grey] Leave an offering at the lake's edge and pray.

**Demon Checkpoint** — *A wobbly table blocks the path. Behind it, a bureaucratic demon in a stained uniform holds a quill the size of a sword. "Halt. Passage requires documentation."*
